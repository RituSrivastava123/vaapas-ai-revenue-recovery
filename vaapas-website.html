The problem

A fixed retry has no idea when your salary clears.

Most failed-payment recovery in India is English-first and one-shot: an SMS with a link, sent on a fixed schedule, to a customer who thinks in Hinglish. It never asks why the payment failed, and it never asks when the money will actually be there — so retries land on the 24th when the salary comes on the 10th, and links go unread.

How it works

Six stages. The model only ever gets to advise.

Diagnosis, negotiation and extraction feed a promise into the pipeline. Only one module — policy_gate.py, zero LLM calls — has the authority to turn that promise into money movement.

1

Failure diagnosis

A rule-based RCA engine reads the error code — bank timeout, insufficient funds, expired mandate, OTP drop-off — and proposes why the payment failed.

advises only
2

Hinglish negotiation

The agent asks when and how much the customer can pay, in the register they actually use — up to three genuine back-and-forth turns, not one generated line.

advises only
3

Promise extraction

A separate pass turns the transcript into a strict schema: status, date, amount, confidence. Kept apart from negotiation so politeness — "dekh lenge" — never gets read as a commitment.

advises only
4

Policy validation

The extracted promise — including its confidence score — is treated as an unverified claim. Amount, date, retry caps, cooldowns and stop conditions are checked against hard, deterministic rules.

sole authority
5

Authorized action

One of five outcomes: retry scheduled, retry deferred, escalated to a human, stopped, or rejected as invalid. A retry being authorized is not the same event as it being recovered.

sole authority
6

Simulated outcome

A labeled, synthetic payment-outcome model decides whether the scheduled retry would actually have been paid — so the benchmark reports something closer to revenue than to promise-counting.

measured, not assumed

The invariant

No upstream model output can directly authorize a payment action.

policy_gate.py has zero imports of any model client. Every rule in it — retry caps, cooldowns, a discount ceiling, a sanity ceiling, stale-date rejection, conflicting-commitment escalation, duplicate-event idempotency, STOP and fraud hard-stops — is independently unit-tested, 26 tests deep.

Negotiation & extraction layer

  • Understand the customer's Hinglish
  • Negotiate a date and amount
  • Extract {status, date, amount, confidence}
  • Report a confidence score

Policy gate — zero LLM calls

  • Decide whether a retry is financially allowed
  • Enforce amount bounds and a hard sanity ceiling
  • Enforce retry limits and cooldowns
  • Respect STOP / fraud unconditionally, first
  • Authorize execution — the only module that can
